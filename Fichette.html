ALERT TRIGGERING (GLOBAL)

The model detects progressive loss of R134a refrigerant charge through the condenser by monitoring thermodynamic instability in the closed-loop vapor cycle during active operation. When refrigerant escapes, the compressor experiences low suction pressure and severe discharge overheating with elevated rolling variance. The algorithm registers a flight-level abnormal event whenever pressure and temperature limits are simultaneously breached during valid operating periods. When more than 4 abnormal flight events accumulate within a sliding window of 10 valid flights, the model triggers an alert in SPM.

ALERT TRIGGERING (DETAILED)

The mathematical detection framework operates in three sequential computational tiers:

Tier 1: Flight Validity & Precondition Assessment
A flight is classified as valid for feature calculation if and only if:
• T_coolant_return (CDM 1) > 0 °C
• Speed_CDM_pump > 100 rpm
• Speed_VCRU_compressor > 1000 rpm
(If unfulfilled, Flight is tagged "System not operated" or "Invalid conditions"; event computation is skipped).
Tier 2: Single-Flight Abnormal Event Logic
Within each valid flight, rolling 10-minute statistics are evaluated across time-series data. An Abnormal Event (Event_k = 1) is flagged if all four thermodynamic criteria are met:
1. VCRU Inlet Pressure < 1.5 bar
2. σ_10min(VCRU Inlet Pressure) > 1.0 bar
3. VCRU Compressor Outlet Temperature > 90 °C
4. σ_10min(VCRU Compressor Outlet Temperature) > 15 °C
Where rolling standard deviation over a 10-minute window of M samples is defined as:
σ_10min(X) = sqrt( (1 / M) * Σ [X_j - μ_X]^2 )
Tier 3: Multi-Flight Sliding Window & Triggering Policy
Alert state is computed across a sliding FIFO window of N = 10 valid flights:
Cumulative_Events = Σ [Event_k] for k = 1 to 10
Alert Condition: Cumulative_Events > 4
(Strictly greater than 4 abnormal events; alert fires on the 5th event within 10 valid flights).

MODEL BEHAVIOR
  • Ground vs. Flight Operational Discrepancy: In-service PFR telemetry demonstrates that leaking VCRUs frequently start up normally during ground turnaround but trip off or degrade rapidly during flight under steady thermal loads.
  • High Ground Ambient Temperature Sensitivity: Loss of chilling efficiency is significantly magnified in tropical/hot weather environments, rapidly generating ACU cabin alerts.
  • Fleet Vulnerability in Dual-VCRU Architecture: Aircraft configured with only two VCRUs exhibit acute operational vulnerability, making VCRU failure the #1 Operational Interruption driver on A350-1000.
  • Rolling Standard Deviation Filtering: The 10-minute statistical window effectively filters out momentary transient fluctuations during compressor spin-up, preventing false single-point triggers.
  • Counter Persistence: The cumulative counter retains historical memory over the 10-valid-flight window, requiring at least 6 consecutive nominal flights to clear the alert state naturally.
MODEL LIMITATIONS
  • Strict Airline SAR 06 Data Dependency: The model is completely non-operational for airlines that do not record and transmit SAR Channel 06 (model was validated exclusively on SIA, QTR, CPA, and FIN fleets).
  • High False Alarm / Over-Alerting Volume in Feasibility: Feasibility testing across 62,424 flights identified alerts on 120 out of 136 aircraft (30% of total flights produced alerts), necessitating threshold re-tuning prior to airline deployment.
  • Inactive Unit Blindness: If a VCRU is locked out, switched off, or runs below 1000 rpm, validity preconditions fail and the algorithm cannot assess refrigerant levels.
  • Anticipation Horizon vs. Detection: The algorithm is primarily a diagnostic/reactive detector of active thermodynamic collapse rather than a long-horizon prognostic tool for predicting micro-leaks before fluid loss.
  • Refrigerant vs. Coolant Distinction: The model strictly detects internal R134a refrigerant leakage within the VCRU; it does NOT monitor external Propylene Glycol Water (PGW) coolant distribution loop leaks.
